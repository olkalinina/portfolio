“Not converting” is a symptom, not a diagnosis

Your analytics can show where people leave. They rarely show what a first-time visitor misunderstood, doubted, or couldn’t find.

When you know your product well, it’s hard to see it as a stranger does. A page can look complete to the team that built it and still leave a new visitor with unanswered questions.

The same symptom can have very different causes, and each needs a different response.

Visitors don’t convert

  1. Comprehension

    The visitor can’t quickly tell what the product does or how it helps.

    Do they understand it?

  2. Trust

    They don’t have enough reason to believe the product or its claims.

    Do they believe it?

  3. Fit

    The offer doesn’t connect to their situation or needs.

    Is it for them?

  4. Desire

    The value isn’t compelling enough to motivate the next step.

    Do they want it enough?

  5. Missing information

    An answer, step, or piece of guidance they need is absent or arrives too late.

    What’s missing, or late?

A real finding, from a case study

  1. Observable issueTwo neighboring homepage cards say “zero commission” and “1% commission”, and nothing says who each applies to.
  2. Visitor perceptionThe platform can’t clearly explain what it charges.
  3. Decision barrierTrust in the core promise of no agent fees is weakened before anything else is evaluated.
  4. Conversion consequenceDirect visitors leave early. The report treats this as a plausible driver, not a proven one.
  5. DirectionName the audience on each card and set the 1% against the typical agent fee.

Questions before you start

What is a CRO audit?

A structured review of a website or digital product to find what keeps visitors from completing a desired action. It covers the journey, messaging, trust, and the behavioral evidence available, and ends in prioritized recommendations.

How is a CRO audit different from a UX audit?

A UX audit looks at usability and the overall experience. A CRO audit looks at the barriers to a specific outcome, such as sign-ups, purchases, or demo requests. The two overlap. Read the full comparison.

Do I need to give you analytics access?

No. Analytics such as GA4 can strengthen the diagnosis: they show where people leave and which areas deserve a closer look, so share them if you have them. Without them, I examine the journey and the product experience directly and report what the evidence supports. Findings reflect the evidence available.

Will you tell me how much my conversion will rise?

No. I don’t promise a specific uplift or invent projections. I identify and explain conversion barriers within the agreed scope.

Will you rewrite my copy or redesign my pages?

Not as part of the audit. The report gives direction and priorities. If you want help with the redesign, see my product design services.

Will you implement the changes or check the results?

No. Implementation and post-change measurement are outside the audit. Your team can use the report to decide what to change and test.

Do you advise on product strategy, pricing, or market fit?

No. I examine how the product presents its offer, including how pricing is presented, not the underlying business model or market fit.

What kinds of products do you take on?

SaaS products, AI-powered products, digital platforms, marketplaces, e-commerce, and other digital services.

Does it work for a product with low traffic?

Yes, for what it examines. A diagnosis can still find messaging, trust, and usability problems, although limited traffic restricts what analytics can confirm.
