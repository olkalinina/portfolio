Behavioral CRO Diagnosis

CRO audit: understand why users aren't converting

Your website or digital product may be losing potential customers for reasons that aren't immediately obvious. A CRO audit examines where users encounter friction, what undermines trust, and what prevents them from taking the next step.

Behavioral CRO Diagnosis combines product UX analysis, behavioral reasoning and available analytics to identify conversion barriers and determine what is worth improving first.

What the diagnosis investigates

The scope depends on the product, its goals and the challenges being investigated. The analysis can cover the following areas.

01

Value proposition

Whether visitors understand what the product offers, who it is for, and why it matters to them.

02

Trust and credibility

What helps users trust the product and its offer — and what creates doubt or makes the decision feel risky.

03

Conversion journey

Where obstacles emerge between the first interaction and the intended action, from exploring an offer to signing up or making a purchase.

04

Onboarding and activation

What makes it difficult for users to get started, complete key steps and experience the product's value.

05

Decision friction

What prevents users from choosing a plan, requesting a demo, signing up, purchasing or continuing through an important flow.

06

UX and interaction issues

How confusing navigation, unclear messaging, unnecessary steps or inconsistent interface patterns may interfere with the user's ability to complete a task.

Who it's for

CRO audits are particularly useful for businesses that have an existing website or digital product and need to understand why users aren't progressing as expected.

Suitable for

  • SaaS products
  • AI-powered products
  • Digital platforms
  • Marketplaces
  • E-commerce
  • Other digital services

Common situations include

  • Traffic exists, but conversion rates are below expectations.
  • Visitors explore the offer but don't sign up, request a demo or purchase.
  • Users abandon important steps in a signup or onboarding flow.
  • The value proposition is difficult to communicate.
  • The product has accumulated UX issues, but the team doesn't know which ones matter most.
  • The team has analytics data but needs help interpreting what may be preventing conversion.

A CRO audit is most useful when there is a defined conversion goal and enough product context to investigate the experience. Available analytics and behavioral data can strengthen the diagnosis, but the scope can be adapted when data is limited.

How the CRO audit works

  1. 01

    Context

    Establish the product's goals, target audience, conversion objectives and current challenges. Review the available product information, analytics and relevant business context.

  2. 02

    Investigation

    Examine the interface and key user journeys. Depending on the project, the analysis may include analytics data, session recordings, heatmaps, competitor reviews and other relevant evidence.

  3. 03

    Diagnosis

    Identify conversion barriers, assess their potential behavioral implications and distinguish directly observable issues from hypotheses that require further validation.

  4. 04

    Action plan

    Translate findings into prioritized recommendations. Each recommendation explains what should be improved, why it matters and what the team can investigate or change next.

The analysis distinguishes observed evidence from reasoned hypotheses. Conclusions are calibrated to the information available; assumptions are not presented as proven user behavior.

What you'll receive

A structured CRO audit report designed to help the team understand the problems and decide what to address first.

  • 01Evidence-backed findingsSupported by annotated screenshots and relevant data where available.
  • 02Behavioral explanationsDescribing how each identified issue may affect user understanding, confidence or decision-making.
  • 03Prioritized recommendationsBased on the likely impact, strength of evidence and practical relevance of each issue.
  • 04Implementation directionExplaining what to change and how the improvement could be approached.
  • 05A practical action planThat helps the team determine what to address first and what may require further investigation or testing.

The deliverable is a diagnostic report and a clear direction for improvement — not just a list of interface problems.

Beyond the checklist

Conversion diagnosis that goes beyond identifying what's wrong.

Standard audit

A checklist tells you what's wrong.

A conventional audit may flag usability issues, unclear copy, weak calls to action, or missing trust signals.

It identifies potential problems against established criteria, but may not explain their underlying causes, their significance in the context of the product, or what should be addressed first.

Example findings

  • CTA lacks contrast
  • Value proposition is unclear
  • Trust signals are missing

Behavioral CRO Diagnosis

A diagnosis explains why it matters — and what to do about it.

I investigate the reasons users may hesitate, abandon a journey, or fail to convert. I connect observable issues to their behavioral implications, evaluate their significance in context, and develop practical recommendations for improvement.

The result is not just a list of issues. It's a reasoned diagnosis and a clear direction for what to improve first.

Example diagnostic sequence

  1. Observed issueThe offer doesn't explain the value clearly.
  2. Behavioral implication HypothesisUsers may struggle to assess relevance.
  3. Recommended directionClarify the value in relation to user needs.

Illustrative examples, not findings from a client project. In a real diagnosis, behavioral implications are labelled as hypotheses unless the evidence supports them.

Frequently asked questions

What is a CRO audit?

A conversion rate optimization audit is a structured review of a website or digital product to identify issues that may prevent users from completing a desired action. It examines the conversion journey, user experience, messaging, trust and relevant behavioral evidence to produce prioritized recommendations.

How is a CRO audit different from a UX audit?

A UX audit focuses on usability and the overall user experience. A CRO audit focuses on barriers to a specific business outcome, such as signups, purchases or demo requests. Product usability is one of the factors that can influence conversion.

Is analytics data required?

Analytics data, session recordings and heatmaps can strengthen the diagnosis, especially when they help identify drop-off points or patterns in user behavior. If these sources are unavailable, the scope can be based on the information that is accessible. Findings will reflect the strength and limitations of the available evidence.

What does the CRO audit report include?

The report includes documented findings, supporting evidence where available, explanations of potential behavioral implications, prioritized recommendations and an action plan. The exact scope depends on the product and the questions being investigated.

Does the service include redesign or implementation?

The core service focuses on diagnosis and recommendations. Product design, interface redesign and implementation support can be scoped separately based on the findings and the team's needs.

Can a CRO audit guarantee a conversion increase?

No audit can guarantee a specific uplift. Results depend on the product, audience, traffic, implementation and other factors. The purpose of the diagnosis is to identify plausible barriers, explain the evidence and establish a more informed direction for improvement.

Is a CRO audit suitable for a product with low traffic?

A diagnosis may still identify usability, messaging and trust issues, but limited traffic can restrict what analytics data can establish. The scope and confidence of conclusions should reflect the available evidence.

How much does a CRO audit cost?

The cost depends on the product's complexity, the number of flows or pages being investigated, the available data and the required depth of analysis. The scope and estimate can be discussed after an initial review of the project.
